## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the decision governance model for the AIS programme. It defines how decisions of all kinds are classified, who has authority to make them, what format they must follow, how they are escalated, and what audit requirements ensure traceability and accountability. All programme decisions must be recorded and governed in accordance with this model.

The model ensures:

- Every significant decision is explicitly captured and cannot be silently made.
- Authority boundaries are clear and enforced.
- Decisions are traceable to programme principles and invariants.
- An immutable audit trail is maintained for compliance and review.

---

## 2. Decision Categories

All programme decisions fall into one of the following categories:

### 2.1 Constitutional Decisions
Decisions that establish, amend, or revoke programme-level principles, invariants, or foundational rules. These are the most consequential decisions and require the highest authority.

### 2.2 Architectural Decisions (ADR)
Decisions that affect cross-domain architecture, integration patterns, shared infrastructure, or technology selections that impact more than one domain. Recorded as Architecture Decision Records (ADRs).

### 2.3 Domain Decisions (DADR)
Decisions scoped to a single domain — entity changes, invariant updates, behaviour modifications, or domain model evolution. Recorded as Domain Architecture Decision Records (DADRs).

### 2.4 Security Decisions
Decisions related to threat models, access controls, encryption, compliance obligations, or vulnerability management that affect the programme or any domain.

### 2.5 Governance Decisions
Decisions about the governance framework itself — process changes, authority delegation, audit policy updates, or document lifecycle rules.

### 2.6 Implementation Decisions
Decisions about coding standards, tooling, CI/CD configuration, test strategies, or other delivery-level concerns that do not alter architecture or domain models.

## 3. Decision Framework (D1–D9)

The decision levels defined in CON-001 are mapped here to authority and required outputs:

| Level | Scope | Authority | Required Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** | Individual code-level choice | Implementer | None (follows existing ADR/DADR) |
| **D2** | Task-local implementation detail | Task Lead | Implementation note in PR |
| **D3** | Domain-internal design choice | Domain Owner | DADR (Draft) |
| **D4** | Cross-domain integration pattern | Architecture Board | ADR (Draft) |
| **D5** | Domain model change (entity/invariant) | Domain Owner + Architecture Review | DADR (Approved) |
| **D6** | Architectural principle or shared standard | Architecture Board + Programme Lead | ADR (Approved) |
| **D7** | Programme invariant or principle change | Programme Steering Committee | ADR (Approved) + CON amendment |
| **D8** | Governance framework change | Programme Steering Committee | GOV amendment (Approved) |
| **D9** | Programme charter or mission change | Executive Sponsor | Charter amendment (Approved) |

### 3.1 Authority Mapping Rules

- Decisions at D3 and below may be made within the domain without external approval, provided the decision does not violate existing invariants or principles.
- Decisions at D4–D6 require Architecture Board review and consensus.
- Decisions at D7–D9 require Steering Committee or Executive approval and may trigger baseline version increments.
- No decision level may be bypassed; if scope is uncertain, the higher level applies.

## 4. ADR Format Template

All Architecture Decision Records must follow this structure:

```
ADR-NNN: [Title]

Status: [Proposed | Accepted | Deprecated | Superseded by ADR-MMM]
Date: YYYY-MM-DD
Decision Level: [D4 | D5 | D6 | D7]
Author(s): [Name(s)]

## Context
[Description of the architectural concern, driving forces, and
constraints that led to this decision. Reference any relevant
principles (CON-001), existing ADRs, or external standards.]

## Decision
[The specific decision made, stated clearly and unambiguously.]

## Consequences
[Positive and negative outcomes of the decision. Include impact
on performance, maintainability, security, and cross-domain
coupling.]

## Compliance
- [ ] Consistent with CON-001 principles
- [ ] Security review completed (if applicable)
- [ ] Affected domains notified
- [ ] No invariant violations introduced

## Traceability
- Related ADRs: [ADR-XXX, ADR-YYY]
- Related DADRs: [DADR-XXX]
- Related CON sections: [CON-001 §X]
```

## 5. DADR Format Template

All Domain Architecture Decision Records must follow this structure:

```
DADR-NNN: [Title]

Status: [Proposed | Accepted | Deprecated | Superseded by DADR-MMM]
Severity: [S1 | S2 | S3 | S4 | S5]
Domain: [Domain Name]
Date: YYYY-MM-DD
Decision Level: [D3 | D5]
Author(s): [Name(s)]

## Domain Context
[Description of the domain-specific concern, including the
entities and invariants affected, the business driver, and
any constraints from the domain boundary definition.]

## Decision
[The specific decision made within the domain scope.]

## Domain Consequences
[Impact on the domain model, entity lifecycle, invariant
enforcement, event flow, and domain behaviour.]

## Impact Analysis
- Affected Entities: [list]
- Affected Invariants: [list]
- Affected Principles: [list]
- Cross-domain Impact: [None | Description]
- Migration Required: [Yes | No]

## Compliance
- [ ] Entity ownership assigned
- [ ] Invariant implications assessed
- [ ] Domain Owner approved
- [ ] Architecture review completed (if cross-domain)

## Traceability
- Related ADRs: [ADR-XXX, if any]
- Related DADRs: [DADR-XXX, DADR-YYY]
- Related GOV sections: [GOV-XXX]
```

## 6. Escalation Path

When a decision cannot be resolved at its natural level, the following escalation path applies:

### 6.1 Escalation Triggers

- **Disagreement**: The assigned authority cannot reach consensus within 5 business days.
- **Scope Uncertainty**: The decision's scope spans multiple levels and the correct level is disputed.
- **Invariant Conflict**: The decision may violate an existing programme invariant or principle.
- **Authority Override**: The responsible party is unavailable or has a conflict of interest.

### 6.2 Escalation Sequence

1. **Level 1 Escalation**: Raise to the next higher decision level authority (e.g., D3 → D4).
2. **Architecture Board Review**: For D4+ decisions in dispute, the full Architecture Board convenes.
3. **Steering Committee Arbitration**: If the Architecture Board cannot resolve, the Steering Committee decides.
4. **Executive Decision**: For D9-level disputes or programme charter implications, the Executive Sponsor decides.

### 6.3 Escalation Requirements

- All escalations must be documented with the original decision record, including the reason for escalation and the resolution.
- Escalation does not skip levels; each step must be attempted unless explicitly waived by Steering Committee.
- The escalated decision inherits the higher level's output and audit requirements.

## 7. Audit Requirements

### 7.1 Append-Only Log

- All ADRs and DADRs are stored in version control and follow an **append-only** policy.
- Once a decision record is committed, its content **must not be altered**. Corrections are made by issuing a new record that supersedes the original.
- Any attempt to rewrite or delete historical decision records is a governance violation.

### 7.2 Deprecation and Supersession

- A decision record may only be deprecated when a **successor record** has been accepted.
- The deprecated record's status is updated to `Deprecated` or `Superseded by ADR/DADR-NNN` (this status change is the sole permitted modification to a committed record).
- The successor record must explicitly reference the deprecated record in its Traceability section.
- A chain of supersession must be traceable from the current record back to the original.

### 7.3 Audit Trail Requirements

| Requirement | Description |
|---|---|
| **Unique Identifier** | Every ADR/DADR has a unique, monotonically increasing number. |
| **Timestamp** | Every record carries the date of acceptance. |
| **Authorship** | Every record lists its author(s). |
| **Status History** | Status changes are logged with dates. |
| **Version Linking** | Supersession chains are bidirectionally linked. |
| **Review Evidence** | Approval signatures or review board minutes are attached. |

### 7.4 Periodic Audit

- A decision audit shall be conducted at each programme milestone.
- The audit verifies: all outstanding decisions have a current status, no orphaned records exist, supersession chains are complete, and compliance checkboxes are accurate.

---

## 8. Enforcement

- Decisions made without a required ADR/DADR are considered **ungoverned** and must be retroactively documented before the next milestone.
- Repeated governance violations are escalated per Section 6 and may result in authority restrictions.
- This document is governed by GOV-001 and may only be amended per the process defined therein.

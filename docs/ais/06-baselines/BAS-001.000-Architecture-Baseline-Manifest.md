## 0. Introduction

This document is the Architecture Baseline Manifest for AIS Baseline v1.0. It defines the frozen scope, exact composition, version binding, compatibility guarantees, update rules, and sign-off authority for the first formal baseline of the Adaptive Intelligence System.

A baseline is an immutable snapshot of the normative architecture at a point in time. Once declared, the baseline's contents are locked: they may be referenced, depended upon, and built against with the assurance that they will not change without a controlled, visible, and approved process.

This manifest is the definitive inventory of what constitutes AIS Baseline v1.0. Any claim of baseline membership that is not recorded here is invalid.

---

## 1. Baseline Scope

### 1.1 What Is Frozen

AIS Baseline v1.0 freezes the following architectural dimensions:

- **Constitutional framework**: The 23 Constitutional Principles (CP-001 through CP-023) and 12 Architectural Laws (AL-001 through AL-012) that govern all design and implementation.
- **System shape**: The eight Functional Pillars (FP-01 through FP-08), their responsibilities, and their module assignments.
- **Design requirements**: The 11 non-negotiable Design Requirements (DR-01 through DR-11).
- **Trust model**: The five Trust Zones (Z0 through Z4), their boundaries, and the six zone gate rules (G-01 through G-06).
- **Autonomy model**: The five-level Autonomy Spectrum (L0 through L4) and its default.
- **Architectural decisions**: The 13 ADRs (ADR-001.000 through ADR-013.000) that capture individual design choices and their rationale.
- **Interface contracts**: The five core interface contracts (IC-01 through IC-05) as expressed in TypeScript types.
- **Data model outline**: The eight core data entities and their ownership assignments.

### 1.2 What Is Not Frozen

The following are explicitly excluded from baseline scope:

- Implementation code, test suites, and build artefacts.
- Operational runbooks, deployment configurations, and infrastructure as code.
- Plugin implementations and third-party extensions.
- Provider-specific configurations and credentials.
- Domain models (DOM documents), which evolve independently under Domain Council authority.
- Governance operating procedures (GOV documents), which evolve under Governance Council authority.

## 3. Compatibility Statement

### 3.1 Internal Compatibility

All documents within AIS Baseline v1.0 have been reviewed for mutual consistency. The following compatibility constraints are certified:

- **CON-001.000 ↔ ARC-001.001**: ARC-001.001 conforms to every CP and AL in CON-001.000. No contradictions exist between the two documents.
- **CON-001.000 ↔ ADR-001..013**: Every ADR respects the Constitutional Principles and Architectural Laws. Where an ADR introduces an implementation pattern, it traces back to the specific CP or AL it satisfies.
- **ARC-001.001 ↔ ADR-001..013**: The architecture described in ARC-001.001 is consistent with all 13 ADRs. FP assignments, DR constraints, and module architecture align with the decisions recorded in the ADRs.
- **ADR cross-references**: No circular dependencies exist between ADRs. Each ADR references its upstream CP/AL and its downstream effects without contradiction.

### 3.2 External Compatibility

AIS Baseline v1.0 is compatible with the following external constraints:

- **TypeScript ecosystem**: Interface contracts (IC-01 through IC-05) are expressible in TypeScript. No contract requires a language feature not available in the target TypeScript version.
- **File system storage**: The file-based persistence model (ADR-004, AL-009) is compatible with standard POSIX file systems and does not require exotic storage backends.
- **Provider independence**: No document requires a specific LLM provider, scanner provider, or storage provider. The Provider Abstraction pattern (ADR-003, AL-005) ensures vendor-neutral operation.

### 3.3 Incompatibility Register

There are no known incompatibilities within AIS Baseline v1.0. If an incompatibility is discovered after baseline declaration, it must be reported through the change request process (§5) and cannot be silently patched.

## 4. Baseline Integrity

### 4.1 Immutable Snapshot

AIS Baseline v1.0 is an immutable snapshot. The 16 documents at their specified versions are frozen. Implementations may depend on them with full assurance of stability.

### 4.2 Hash Verification

Each document in the baseline should have a content hash recorded at the time of baseline declaration. The hash serves as an integrity check: if a document's hash does not match the recorded value, the baseline is considered compromised.

### 4.3 Audit Trail

The act of declaring AIS Baseline v1.0 is itself an auditable event, recorded in the programme's governance log with:

- Date and time of declaration.
- Authorising body (Architecture Council).
- List of documents and their versions.
- Any dissenting opinions or conditions recorded at declaration time.

## 5. Update Rules

### 5.1 General Principle

Baseline contents may only be changed through a formal change request process. Silent edits, "drive-by" modifications, or undocumented changes to any baseline document are **forbidden** by CP-016 (Read-Only Foundation) and CP-018 (Versioned Evolution).

### 5.2 Change Request Process

To modify any document within AIS Baseline v1.0:

1. **Submit a Change Request (CR)**: The requestor creates a CR document specifying the target document(s), the proposed change(s), the rationale, and the impact assessment.
2. **Architecture Council Review**: The Architecture Council evaluates the CR for consistency with higher-tier documents (CON-001.000), impact on dependent ADRs, and alignment with the 23 Constitutional Principles.
3. **Impact Analysis**: Every CR must include an analysis of which other baseline documents are affected by the proposed change. No change is approved in isolation if it creates downstream inconsistencies.
4. **Approval or Rejection**: The Architecture Council approves or rejects the CR. Rejection must include rationale. Approval authorises the issuance of a new document version (e.g., ADR-001.001) and, if necessary, a new baseline version.
5. **New Baseline Declaration**: If approved changes are significant enough to warrant a new baseline, the Architecture Council declares a new baseline version (e.g., AIS Baseline v1.1 or v2.0) with an updated BAS document.

### 5.3 Emergency Amendments

In the event of a critical defect (e.g., a security vulnerability in the trust model, a contradiction between two baseline documents that blocks implementation), the Architecture Council may authorise an emergency amendment:

- The emergency amendment is documented as a CR with expedited review.
- The amended document receives a new version number.
- A post-mortem review is conducted within five business days.
- The emergency amendment is incorporated into the next scheduled baseline revision.

### 5.4 Prohibited Actions

The following actions are explicitly prohibited with respect to AIS Baseline v1.0:

- Editing a baseline document without issuing a new version number.
- Removing a document from the baseline without declaring a new baseline.
- Adding a document to the baseline without declaring a new baseline.
- Changing a document's version binding (e.g., upgrading from ADR-001.000 to ADR-001.001 within the same baseline) without a new baseline declaration.

## 6. Sign-Off Authority

### 6.1 Architecture Council

The Architecture Council is the sole authority for declaring, maintaining, and revoking architecture baselines. Its responsibilities include:

- Reviewing and approving all change requests affecting baseline documents.
- Declaring new baseline versions.
- Certifying internal compatibility across baseline documents.
- Adjudicating conflicts between baseline documents (per CON-001.000 §5).
- Ensuring traceability from every baseline document back to the Constitution.

### 6.2 Sign-Off Record

The declaration of AIS Baseline v1.0 is signed off by the following authorities:

| Role | Authority | Date |

| Security Council Liaison | Certifies trust model integrity | 2026-07-28 |
| Domain Council Liaison | Certifies domain traceability | 2026-07-28 |

### 6.3 Delegation

The Architecture Council may delegate routine baseline maintenance (e.g., typo corrections in non-normative sections) to a designated Baseline Steward, provided that:

- The Baseline Steward does not change normative content (CP, AL, DR, FP, Zone definitions, Autonomy levels).
- All changes are reported to the Architecture Council at the next scheduled meeting.
- No change crosses a document boundary without explicit Architecture Council awareness.

## 7. Relationship to Other Baselines

AIS Baseline v1.0 is the first architecture baseline of the AIS programme. It is anticipated that subsequent baselines (v1.1, v1.2, v2.0) will be declared as the architecture evolves. Each new baseline will have its own BAS document (e.g., BAS-001.001, BAS-002.000) and will explicitly reference the baseline it supersedes.

### 7.1 Supersession Chain

```
BAS-001.000 (this document) → AIS Baseline v1.0
    └── superseded by (future): BAS-001.001 → AIS Baseline v1.1
                                       └── superseded by (future): BAS-002.000 → AIS Baseline v2.0
```

### 7.2 Parallel Baselines

Parallel baselines (e.g., a v1.x maintenance branch and a v2.x development branch) are permitted, provided each has its own BAS document and the Architecture Council explicitly authorises parallel operation.
